**A cafe that works for a business meeting in Tokyo needs table seating that allows normal conversation, a location easy to direct a guest to, and an atmosphere that doesn't rush either of you out after one drink.**

A cafe good for solo work and a cafe good for a business meeting aren't automatically the same place. Solo work rewards a quiet counter seat facing a wall; a meeting rewards a table where two people can talk at a normal volume without either straining to hear or worrying the next table is listening in. If you're coordinating with a client or colleague who doesn't know the neighborhood, the directions matter just as much as the room itself.

## What a Business Meeting Actually Needs From a Cafe

A few conditions matter more for a meeting than for solo work:

- **Table seating, not just counter seats.** Facing each other across a table makes a meeting feel like a meeting rather than two strangers who happen to be sitting near each other.
- **A quiet enough room that conversation stays private.** Background noise low enough that your discussion doesn't carry, and the next table's conversation doesn't either.
- **Easy directions for a guest.** If you're meeting someone who hasn't been there before, a cafe with a clear address (even without a visible sign) is easier to direct someone to than a hard-to-describe location buried in a shopping building.
- **No pressure to clear the table quickly.** A meeting can run long once the conversation gets going; a cafe that rushes turnover works against that.
- **Wi-Fi and power, just in case.** Useful for pulling up a document or a deck, even if the meeting itself doesn't need a laptop open the whole time.

## Finding the Right Spot in Kitasando

Green Beans Coffee, in the quiet [Kitasando](/en/articles/discover-kitasando-tokyo/) area near Sendagaya, covers most of this well. The shop has no storefront sign, which sounds like a drawback for meeting a guest, but the address itself (3-30-1 Sendagaya, on the first floor of the RC Apartment building) is straightforward to send ahead of time, and it's about three minutes on foot from Kitasando Station, so directions are short even for someone visiting for the first time. The room stays quiet enough that a normal conversation doesn't carry to neighboring tables, and both counter and table seating are available depending on how many people are meeting.

Every seat has free Wi-Fi and a power outlet, an English menu is available, and the shop takes cashless payment, which covers most of what an international business guest might need without advance explanation. On weekdays, one drink comfortably covers about two hours, so a meeting that runs long isn't a problem.

## A Few Practical Tips

- Send the exact address ahead of time rather than relying on a guest spotting a sign; there isn't one.
- If the meeting involves screen-sharing or documents, confirm Wi-Fi availability in advance even though it's standard here — it's one less thing to worry about on the day.
- Mornings and early afternoons tend to be quieter than the lunch window, which matters if the conversation needs to stay private.
- Hours run 10:30 to 18:30 with last orders at 18:00, so schedule meetings accordingly, especially if dinner plans follow.
