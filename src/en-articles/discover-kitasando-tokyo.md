**Kitasando is a small, quiet pocket of Shibuya, Tokyo, wedged between the energy of Shinjuku and Harajuku — and one of the easiest ways to get to know it is over a cup of coffee at Green Beans Coffee, a shop with no sign out front.**

Most visitors reach Tokyo's bigger neighborhoods first: Shinjuku, Harajuku, Shibuya. Kitasando rarely makes that list, which is exactly why I like sending people here. It's three minutes on foot from Kitasando Station, close enough to the National Olympic Stadium and Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium to combine with an event day, and quiet enough that a short walk feels like a break from the rest of the city.

## Where Kitasando Fits on the Map

The area sits in what locals call Dagayasando, a loosely defined pocket that blends into Sendagaya. It's about a seven-minute walk from Sendagaya Station and roughly ten minutes from Shinjuku by train, with Yoyogi and Harajuku both within easy reach. Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine is a short stroll away if you want a quieter stop than Meiji Jingu, and the area around the National Olympic Stadium makes a natural pairing for anyone in town for an event there. Shinjuku Gyoen, one of Tokyo's larger parks, is also close enough to fold into the same afternoon if you want a longer walk.

Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine itself is worth the few minutes it takes to reach — it holds one of Tokyo's oldest Fujizuka, a small Mt. Fuji replica you can actually climb, and sits right next to the Japan Shogi Hall, known for its connection to some of the game's most famous players. It's an easy, low-key stop to add between the station and the cafe.

Compared to its neighbors, Kitasando has almost no big-name shopping or landmarks. What it has instead is a residential, low-rise streetscape where small design studios and independent shops sit beside ordinary apartment buildings — the kind of area that rewards walking slowly rather than checking off sights.

## Finding Green Beans Coffee

This is the part that trips people up: Green Beans Coffee has no storefront sign. It's on the first floor of the RC Apartment building at 3-30-1 Sendagaya, Shibuya, and I still tell first-time visitors to watch the address carefully rather than look for a sign, or they'll walk right past it. Once you're inside, it's a self-roasting specialty coffee shop with more than 30 bean varieties, a gluten-free lunch menu, and seating that [welcomes dogs at every table](/en/articles/dog-friendly-cafe-tokyo/).

The shop is open 10:30 to 18:30 with last orders at 18:00, though it closes on irregular days — checking their Instagram before heading over is the safest bet. It's also worth knowing before you go: free Wi-Fi, power outlets, and an English menu are all available, and the shop takes cashless payment if you'd rather not carry cash.

## A Simple Half-Day Walk

I usually suggest starting at Kitasando Station, stopping at Green Beans Coffee for a [hand-drip coffee](/en/articles/japanese-hand-drip-coffee-tokyo/) and the gluten-free one-plate lunch, then walking toward Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine or continuing on to the National Olympic Stadium if there's something happening there. From Sendagaya Station at the other end, it's an easy continuation toward Shinjuku Gyoen if you want to extend the afternoon, or a short train ride back toward Harajuku and Yoyogi Park if you'd rather end the day somewhere livelier.

Mornings tend to be the quietest time to visit the cafe itself, if you'd like the hand-drip counter mostly to yourself before the lunch crowd arrives.

## Why It's Worth the Detour

Kitasando doesn't photograph the way Shibuya or Harajuku does, and that's part of the appeal. There's no crowd to navigate, no line to wait in, and the walk between the station, the shrine, and the cafe rarely takes more than fifteen minutes end to end. For visitors who've already done the highlights, it's a chance to see a version of Tokyo that's closer to how residents actually spend an afternoon.

## Who This Is For

Travelers who've already covered the obvious Tokyo stops and want a neighborhood that moves at a slower pace, or anyone staying near Shinjuku who wants a short, walkable detour rather than another train transfer. It also works well for anyone combining a visit with pet-friendly plans, since the cafe itself is a comfortable stop either way.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. How do I get to Kitasando from Shinjuku?**<br>
It's about ten minutes from Shinjuku, making Kitasando an easy half-day addition to a Tokyo itinerary without a long detour.

**Q. What's near Green Beans Coffee in Kitasando?**<br>
Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine, the National Olympic Stadium, and Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium are all within walking distance, along with Sendagaya Station about seven minutes away on foot.

**Q. Is Kitasando worth visiting if I only have a few hours?**<br>
Yes. It's compact enough to cover in half a day, with a shrine, a stadium area, and a hidden coffee shop all within a short walk of each other.

**Q. Can I continue on to Shinjuku Gyoen from Kitasando?**<br>
Yes. Shinjuku Gyoen is close enough to add to the same walk if you'd like to extend the afternoon beyond Kitasando and Sendagaya.

**Q. Is English spoken or available at Green Beans Coffee?**<br>
An English menu is available, along with free Wi-Fi and cashless payment, so it's a comfortable stop even without Japanese.

## Conclusion

Kitasando won't show up on a typical first-time Tokyo itinerary, but that's part of its appeal. Between Shibuya and Shinjuku, close to Harajuku and Yoyogi, it's a neighborhood built for slower exploring — and Green Beans Coffee, sign or no sign, is a good reason to start there.
