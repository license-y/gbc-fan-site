**Kitasando is a small, quiet pocket of Shibuya, Tokyo, wedged between the energy of Shinjuku and Harajuku — and one of the easiest ways to get to know it is over a cup of coffee at Green Beans Coffee, a shop with no sign out front.**

Most visitors reach Tokyo's bigger neighborhoods first: Shinjuku, Harajuku, Shibuya. Kitasando rarely makes that list, which is exactly why I like sending people here. It's three minutes on foot from Kitasando Station, close enough to the National Olympic Stadium and Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium to combine with an event day, and quiet enough that a short walk feels like a break from the rest of the city.

## Where Kitasando Fits on the Map

The area sits in what locals call Dagayasando, a loosely defined pocket that blends into Sendagaya. It's about a seven-minute walk from Sendagaya Station and roughly ten minutes from Shinjuku by train, with Yoyogi and Harajuku both within easy reach. Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine is a short stroll away if you want a quieter stop than Meiji Jingu, and the area around the National Olympic Stadium makes a natural pairing for anyone in town for an event there.

## Finding Green Beans Coffee

This is the part that trips people up: Green Beans Coffee has no storefront sign. It's on the first floor of the RC Apartment building at 3-30-1 Sendagaya, Shibuya, and I still tell first-time visitors to watch the address carefully rather than look for a sign, or they'll walk right past it. Once you're inside, it's a self-roasting specialty coffee shop with more than 30 bean varieties, a gluten-free lunch menu, and seating that welcomes dogs at every table.

The shop is open 10:30 to 18:30 with last orders at 18:00, though it closes on irregular days — checking their Instagram before heading over is the safest bet.

## A Simple Half-Day Walk

I usually suggest starting at Kitasando Station, stopping at Green Beans Coffee for a hand-drip coffee and the gluten-free one-plate lunch, then walking toward Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine or continuing on to the National Olympic Stadium if there's something happening there. From Sendagaya Station at the other end, it's an easy continuation toward Shinjuku Gyoen if you want to extend the afternoon.

## Who This Is For

Travelers who've already covered the obvious Tokyo stops and want a neighborhood that moves at a slower pace, or anyone staying near Shinjuku who wants a short, walkable detour rather than another train transfer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. How do I get to Kitasando from Shinjuku?**<br>
It's about ten minutes from Shinjuku, making Kitasando an easy half-day addition to a Tokyo itinerary without a long detour.

**Q. What's near Green Beans Coffee in Kitasando?**<br>
Hatonomori Hachiman Shrine, the National Olympic Stadium, and Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium are all within walking distance, along with Sendagaya Station about seven minutes away on foot.

**Q. Is Kitasando worth visiting if I only have a few hours?**<br>
Yes. It's compact enough to cover in half a day, with a shrine, a stadium area, and a hidden coffee shop all within a short walk of each other.

## Conclusion

Kitasando won't show up on a typical first-time Tokyo itinerary, but that's part of its appeal. Between Shibuya and Shinjuku, close to Harajuku and Yoyogi, it's a neighborhood built for slower exploring — and Green Beans Coffee, sign or no sign, is a good reason to start there.
