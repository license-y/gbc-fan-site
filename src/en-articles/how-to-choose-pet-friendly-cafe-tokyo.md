**A genuinely pet-friendly cafe in Tokyo welcomes your dog indoors at any seat, not just an outdoor terrace, and has staff and space set up with pets in mind.**

"Pet-friendly" gets used loosely enough in Tokyo that it's worth checking a few things before you commit to a visit, especially if you're coming from out of town or don't want to find out at the door that your dog has to wait outside. After spending a fair amount of time in cafes around Kitasando and Sendagaya, here's what I'd actually check.

## What "Pet-Friendly" Should Mean

At minimum, a pet-friendly cafe should allow your dog inside the building, not just on an outdoor patio. Beyond that, the useful distinctions are: whether pets are allowed at every seat or only a designated pet section, whether there's a size limit on dogs, and whether the space is actually set up for pets (water bowls, enough room between tables) or just tolerates them.

## Indoor Access vs. Outdoor-Only "Pet-Friendly"

This is the biggest gap between cafes that call themselves pet-friendly. Tokyo's climate swings between humid summers and cold winters, so a cafe that only allows pets on an outdoor terrace is a very different experience from one that allows pets anywhere inside. If you're planning around weather at all, confirm indoor seating specifically rather than assuming "pet-friendly" covers it.

## Size Limits and Seating Rules

Some cafes cap dog size (often small or medium breeds only) or restrict pets to specific tables or a separate room. If you have a large dog, it's worth checking this in advance rather than finding out on arrival. A cafe where every seat, not just a few designated spots, accepts dogs of any size gives you far more flexibility, especially during busier hours when seating is limited.

## Amenities Worth Checking For

A few small details tend to separate a cafe that's genuinely set up for pets from one that simply doesn't object to them:

- **Water bowls** available on loan for dogs who didn't bring one
- **Enough space between tables** so dogs aren't constantly brushing past other customers
- **Calm noise levels**, since a loud, crowded room tends to make nervous dogs more anxious
- **Staff who are used to it**, rather than visibly uneasy around pets
