**Green Beans Coffee in Kitasando, Tokyo, is set up for an actual sit-down lunch with your dog, not just a coffee while your dog waits outside. Every seat is indoors and pet-friendly, and the kitchen serves a proper gluten-free lunch, not just pastries.**

A lot of "dog-friendly" listings turn out to mean a terrace table and a black coffee. I wanted to write about the version where you can actually order a meal, sit for a while, and have your dog settled next to you the whole time — because that's the visit I keep coming back for with my own dog, Pon.

## Why Lunch Is a Different Visit Than Just Coffee

A coffee stop is quick: you're in and out, and your dog barely has time to settle before you're moving again. Lunch is a different pace. You're there for 45 minutes to an hour, which means your dog needs to actually get comfortable, not just tolerate the visit — and the cafe needs food worth sitting down for. Green Beans Coffee works for this because it's a specialty coffee shop with a real kitchen, not a cafe that added a sandwich to the menu as an afterthought.

## What's Actually on the Menu

The gluten-free one-plate lunch is the main thing to know about: a main dish, a side, and a sauce, served with house-made rice flour focaccia instead of regular bread. It's a proper plate, not a snack plate you order because nothing else fits your diet.

One detail that makes lunch an easier decision: if you order before 3 PM, drinks are half price for the rest of your visit. That alone can turn a quick stop into a longer one, especially if your dog has just settled into a good spot under the table and nobody's in a hurry to leave.

I haven't been able to confirm a dog-specific food or treat menu, so I'm not going to claim one exists here — if that matters for your visit, it's worth asking staff directly or checking [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/green.beanscoffee) beforehand.

## Getting Your Dog Settled Before the Food Arrives

The easiest lunches are the ones where your dog is calm before the plates show up, so I usually build in a few extra minutes at the start:

- **Arrive a little before you're hungry** — let your dog sniff around and settle before you order, rather than ordering the second you sit down
- **Ask for a corner or window seat** if your dog is more relaxed with its back to a wall
- **Bring a leash you can shorten** — the tables aren't far apart, and a shorter leash keeps your dog closer while you eat
- Water bowls are provided, so you don't need to bring your own

## Who This Visit Suits

This is the version of a dog-friendly cafe for people who want to actually eat, not just caffeinate — a late breakfast before a walk around Dagayasando, a lunch stop after visiting the National Olympic Stadium area, or a slower weekend meal where you're not watching the clock.
