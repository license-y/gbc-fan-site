Dog-Friendly Cafe in Tokyo

Green Beans Coffee is a house-roasted specialty coffee shop tucked into the quiet Dagayasando neighborhood, between Kitasando and Sendagaya in Shibuya, Tokyo.
There's no storefront sign — it's the kind of place regulars find by word of mouth, and dogs are welcome at every seat.

In many Tokyo cafes, "pet-friendly" means a terrace seat only — fine on a clear afternoon, but not when it's raining or 35°C in August. Green Beans Coffee is different: the whole indoor space is open to dogs of all breeds and sizes, including large dogs, not just an outdoor terrace.

It's a working specialty coffee shop first. The coffee isn't an afterthought to the dog-friendly policy — it's house-roasted from 30+ beans, and the menu includes gluten-free options like rice-flour focaccia.

Indoor Pet-Friendly Seating

30 seats in total — 4 at the counter and 26 at tables — and every one of them is open to dogs.

Coffee & Dining

Dog-friendly doesn't mean the coffee takes a back seat. Green Beans Coffee is a specialty coffee shop that happens to welcome dogs — not a dog cafe that happens to serve coffee.

House-roasted specialty coffee

30+ beans roasted in-house, hand-dripped to order. Ask staff about the day's lineup — flavor profiles range from bright and fruity to deep and chocolatey.

Gluten-free menu

Rice-flour focaccia and other gluten-free dishes are available, so you can eat a real meal while your dog rests beside you — not just grab a coffee and go.

A dog-specific food or treat menu is not confirmed at this time. If that matters for your visit, please ask staff directly via Instagram before you come.

Pet Photo Experiences

One detail that surprises first-time visitors: the wall of instant-film photos of other customers' dogs.

Cheki is the Japanese name for instant film (similar to Polaroid or Instax) — a physical photo that prints on the spot, instead of staying on a phone screen.

When the cafe isn't busy, staff can take a cheki photo of you and your dog and pin it up on the display wall. It's offered free of charge, but it depends on how busy the cafe is at the time — it's worth asking when you arrive, and confirming on Instagram beforehand if a photo matters to your visit.

Visiting with Your Dog

General etiquette for a smooth first visit — same ground rules as most indoor dog-friendly spaces in Japan.

Before you go

  • Keep vaccination records up to date
  • Bring a leash you can shorten indoors
  • Visit outside peak hours if your dog is nervous around others

While you're there

  • Keep your dog leashed at all times
  • Let your dog settle at its own pace around other dogs
  • Ask staff if you'd like help with seating or water

If your dog is still settling in — a recent rescue, or simply shy around new places — a quieter time slot and a short first visit tend to work well.

FAQ

Is Green Beans Coffee dog-friendly indoors, not just on a terrace?
Yes. Every seat inside Green Beans Coffee welcomes dogs, not just an outdoor terrace. It's a specialty coffee shop in Kitasando, Shibuya, Tokyo, with no storefront sign.
Are large dogs allowed inside Green Beans Coffee?
Yes. Dogs of all breeds and sizes, including large dogs, are welcome inside Green Beans Coffee.
Do I need a reservation to bring my dog?
A reservation isn't required, but it's recommended if you'd like a specific seat or are visiting at a busy time. You can reserve via Instagram DM, phone, or Tabelog.
Can I get a photo with my dog?
Yes. When the cafe isn't busy, staff can take an instant-film (cheki) photo of you and your dog and display it in the cafe, free of charge. It depends on how busy the cafe is, so it's worth confirming on the day.
Is there food I can eat while my dog is with me?
Yes. Green Beans Coffee is a specialty coffee shop with 30+ house-roasted beans and a gluten-free menu, including rice-flour focaccia. There is no separate dog-food menu confirmed at this time.
How do I get to Green Beans Coffee?
It's a 3-minute walk from Kitasando Station (Tokyo Metro Fukutoshin Line) and a 7-minute walk from Sendagaya Station (JR Sobu Line), about 10 minutes from Shinjuku.
What should I bring for my dog's first visit?
A leash is required at all times. Water bowls are provided by the cafe, but bringing a familiar blanket or toy can help a nervous dog settle in.
